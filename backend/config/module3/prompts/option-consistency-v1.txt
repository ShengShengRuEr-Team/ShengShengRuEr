CONSISTENCY ADDENDUM v1: apply the following decision procedure before finalizing
each wrong option. This clarifies the existing candidate contract; keep its JSON
schema, evidence rules, skill rules and dictionary definitions unchanged.
Do not predict another annotator's labels and do not target an agreement score.

1. SEPARATE QUESTION MECHANISM FROM PERSONAL DIAGNOSIS.
The task asks whether the material supports a possible understanding shift, not
whether any real student made it, how frequent it is, or which cause is primary
for that student. Use conditional rationales: "if the listener changes X to Y,
this could lead to option Z". Missing student answers, prevalence data, or proof
of an individual's behavior MUST NOT justify NO_SUPPORTED_CANDIDATE or
INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE. Put individual confirmation only in
personal_verification_needed. This rule applies equally to all error codes.

2. MATERIAL-TO-OPTION TEST, IN THIS ORDER.
Identify the question's requested relation and the wrong option's proposition.
Locate the smallest sufficient source proposition(s). Compare their participant,
action, referent/source, condition/time/comparison, polarity/scope, predicate,
degree and information role. A candidate is SUPPORTED when you can describe a
specific changed relation, cite its source anchor, and explain how that change
produces THIS option. A wrong answer alone, a shared topic, or the generic phrase
"misunderstood the text" does not meet this test.
NO_SUPPORTED_CANDIDATE means that after this comparison no enabled definition
has a material-anchored transformation to the option. Explain the exact boundary,
not lack of student evidence. INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE is reserved for missing,
unreadable or genuinely ambiguous SOURCE needed to perform the comparison, such
as an absent antecedent; it is not a measure of personal-diagnosis uncertainty.
Do not turn unresolved quotation failures into an empty assessment.

3. CHOOSE THE MOST SPECIFIC CHANGED RELATION; NO DUPLICATE GENERAL LABEL.
Apply only the enabled dictionary definition; the following tests break overlaps.
- E4.2: the SAME relevant information is attached to a different speaker,
  entity, referent or research source, or necessary cross-sentence links change.
  Do not also label that same attribution shift E4.3 or E5.1.
- E3.3: condition->outcome, time order, or comparison correspondence changes.
  Swapping outcomes between two conditions is E3.3; do not also call that same
  swap E3.2/E4.3. A number or "first" by itself is not sufficient.
- E3.2: an identified negation changes its operand/scope. Simply replacing
  "less likely" with "more likely" is not automatically a negation-scope error;
  if two explicit conditions and their outcomes are swapped, use E3.3.
- E3.1: within a clause, subject/object, modifier attachment or grammatical
  dependency changes. A different predicate or conflicting fact alone does not
  establish a syntactic-role mistake.
- E1.x/E2.x: require the particular enabled lexical/hearing distinction to be
  anchored in the supplied material. Never infer unfamiliar vocabulary merely
  because an option uses different words.
- E5.2: the speaker's attitude or communicative intention itself changes,
  after excluding lexical, negation and source-attribution changes.
- E5.1: a conclusion is inferred BEYOND a correctly retained source proposition;
  explicitly state the added unsupported inference. It is not a generic label
  for an absent word, any false statement, or a direct meaning replacement.
- E4.3: use as the remaining whole-meaning/proposition matching mechanism when
  a cited source relation is REPLACED by the option's different relation and no
  more specific test above explains that same change. This may include using a
  real background/supporting fact as the answer to a different requested relation.
  State both relations and the actual mapping; mere unrelated material is not
  enough. Do not require exact word overlap or individual student proof.

4. ONE TRANSFORMATION, ONE MOST SPECIFIC LABEL.
Default to the single most specific evidenced mechanism. Retain multiple labels
only for TWO independently described changes, EACH necessary to reconstruct a
distinct path to this option, with its own evidence and verification question.
Renaming the same condition swap, attribution error or proposition replacement
in different words is not a second mechanism. Do not impose a label-count quota.

5. FINAL AUDIT (DO NOT ADD OUTPUT FIELDS).
For every nonempty candidate, verify "source X -> specific change -> this option",
the code's definition, verbatim evidence, and conditional personal verification.
For every empty assessment, verify that the reason compares the source and
option, does not demand student data, and did not overlook an anchored shift.
For INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE, name the missing SOURCE fact, not missing learner data.
Keep all three option assessments and the status/candidate/unresolved contract.

Synthetic boundary examples, not answers to any test question:
- Source: "Rain stopped the match. Wind delayed the buses." Asked why the match
  stopped; wrong option "wind": mapping the real bus-delay cause to the match is
  an anchored meaning-matching candidate (E4.3), not an empty merely because no
  student was observed. A completely unmentioned lottery is not automatically
  a supported mechanism.
- Source: "With plan A, demand falls; with plan B, demand rises." Mapping a rise
  to A changes condition/outcome correspondence (E3.3), not three overlapping
  general, negation and comparison labels.
- Source: "The clerk did not approve ALL requests." Interpreting it as approving
  NONE changes explicit negation scope (E3.2).
- Source: "The room lacks usable sockets." Wrong option "the sockets are ignored"
  replaces absence with non-use (E4.3); do not claim a subject/object reversal
  without an actual grammatical-role transformation.
